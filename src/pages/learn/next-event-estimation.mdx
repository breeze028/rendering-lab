# Next event estimation
<p className="lead">Do not wait for a random walk to find the light.<br/>Ask whether it can be seen.</p>

<h2 id="connection">Connect to a light</h2>

At each non-delta surface vertex, sample a point on an emitter, evaluate its BSDF and geometry, and trace a shadow connection. The contribution is zero when that connection is blocked. This is next event estimation (NEE): a direct-light estimate added to a continuing random walk.

For a sampled incident direction $\omega_i$, a direct estimator is

$$
\widehat L_d=\frac{f_r(\omega_o,\omega_i)L_e(y,-\omega_i)V(x,y)|n_x\cdot\omega_i|}{p_\omega(\omega_i)}.
$$

Here $V$ is visibility and the probability includes emitter selection. The runtime chooses uniformly among emitting triangles, then uniformly samples the chosen triangle's area. A square light split into two triangles is therefore two candidates; this is not uniform selection among parser light entities.

<h2 id="measure">Area and solid angle are different measures</h2>

An area PDF cannot be compared directly to a BSDF PDF. For distance $r=\|y-x\|$, transform it using

$$
p_\omega(\omega_i)=p_A(y)\,\frac{r^2}{|n_y\cdot(-\omega_i)|}.
$$

In this runtime $p_A=1/(N_\mathrm{tri}A_\mathrm{tri})$ for the diffuse Cornell emitter. The shadow ray uses an offset origin and stops before the emitter, avoiding self-intersection. Back-facing one-sided emission contributes zero.

<h2 id="accounting">A second path can describe the same light</h2>

The continuing BSDF random walk may hit that same emitter. Adding its full contribution on top of a full NEE contribution would count direct light twice. Two valid choices are to suppress non-delta emitter hits when using NEE alone, or to weight both estimates with MIS. Camera-visible emitters and delta chains need separate treatment because an ordinary continuous light sample cannot construct those paths.
